* `rhf-fit` and `rhf-explain` carry the lessons of their first real run, a hazard forest on 2,386 waiting-list patients. `rhf-fit` has a cleanup section before the fit: `CAPS` caps predictors with implausible tails, and a look-ahead check counts, for events and for the censored, the final intervals on which a predictor changes from the interval before. A dataset build that moves a value from the day of the event back onto the interval ending in it shows as far more changes among events; randomForestRHF assumes each interval's predictors were known when it began and cannot detect this. `LOOKAHEAD_INTERVAL` restricts the check to final intervals of one length, and `LOOKAHEAD <- "reset"` gives each one the predictors of the interval before it, as a sensitivity analysis. Caps and the look-ahead setting are recorded in the forest's lineage. `rhf.rds` is written readable by its owner only, since it holds every patient's intervals. `rhf-explain` explains why its VarPro cache is seeded, that an NA importance means untested rather than unimportant and a 0 means tested with no effect, and that the top tier should be ranked only once it holds across several seeds; it notes that cumulative AUC(t) uses a tree-aggregated cumulative hazard rather than `chf.oob`, why it reports Uno's iAUC rather than `iAUC.std`, and that `chf.oob` and `hazard.oob` rows follow the sorted `ensemble.id`.
