* `vp-fit` now fits VarPro with exploration settings by default, so that it tests as many candidates as it can on a sparse survival cohort. Guided split weights are off (`SPLIT_WEIGHT <- FALSE`, `SPARSE <- FALSE`), so every candidate gets release rules; in a known-signal simulation of 30 candidates, varPro's own lasso screen left about 11 tested at 60 events and 10 at 150. With the weights off, varPro grows its rule forest with `mtry = Inf` and splits binary predictors in about 1 tree in 10, so `MTRY` defaults to `ceiling(sqrt(p))`, which for 30 candidates is 6, tested about 28 of them and recovered the true signals about as well as the defaults did. A survival screen also sets `nodesize.external` to `max(10, round(2 * n / events))`, about two events per leaf in the survival forest varPro grows first (`NODESIZE_EXTERNAL`). The importance figure is now scored on every tree, as the coverage table already was; before this it sampled 150 trees and could disagree with the table. The larger setting for cohorts with roughly 150 events or more (`NTREE`, `MAX_TREE` and `MAX_RULES_TREE` at 1000, 1000 and 300) and varPro's own defaults, kept as the parsimonious screen, are each one uncommented line in the study choices. The report adds that a real effect of a rare binary predictor can be tested and still score z of about 0 at around 60 events, so count the events among the patients who have it, and that a variable correlated with a true signal scores like one. A job scaffolded before this release keeps its old settings until it is upgraded.
